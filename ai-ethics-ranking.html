Ben Explained

1. 🏛️ Governance

For: Keeps AI under control, and helps society adapt. Should also include technical policymaking, i.e. collecting evidence on safety and capabilities for governments to make policy based on. Governance isn’t just about licensing. It could be international treaties, regulation, token taxes, etc.

Against: Concentrates power in existing powerful institutions. Regulatory capture.

Grade: A

2. 🌍 Ethical applied AI

For: Increases mundane utility without increasing capabilities. Domain-specific models go here too, e.g. OlmoEarth.

Against: Not addressing the core issues.

Grade: A−

3. 🔓 Open model development and open science

For: Cuts against concentration of power. Helps defenders. In practice, most of the cyber risk we’ve seen comes from jailbroken closed models, and closed models often can’t be used for defence. Probably guards against obsolescence, because people can use AI at their job instead of being replaced by AI, because it’s not a 1-1 worker replacement if it’s cheap.

Against: Misuse (theoretical), increases capabilities, irreversible.

Grade: A−

4. 📦 Containment

For: Makes AI safe.

Against: Makes AI adoptable, increases automation.

Grade: B+

5. 📏 Evals

For: Understand what’s going on so you can make evidence-based decisions.

Against: Training signal for labs.

Grade: B+

6. 🧭 Alignment

For: Makes AI safe.

Against: Aligned to big companies’ values. Increases capabilities (can’t separate alignment work from finetuning and post-training in general).

Grade: B

7. 😈🔥 Capabilities

Just don’t fucking do this bro, are you serious.

Grade: F− (ask for God’s forgiveness)

My take

Governance and applied AI are the only defensible “it’s just good bro” ones. Alignment work feels icky to me in a way that governance and applied AI don’t.
